# Welcome to the Lost Limb Riders Newsletter

**Brother or Sister,**

Welcome to the Lost Limb Riders family.

If you're reading this because you recently lost a limb, I want you to know something right away:

**You are not alone.**

I know what you're feeling right now. I've been there.

You're looking at what's left and wondering how you're supposed to live the rest of your life like this. You're scared. You're angry. You're frustrated. You're exhausted. And you're probably tired of people telling you, *"It's going to be okay."*

I'm not going to give you some bullshit line and tell you everything will be easy.

I'm going to tell you something better:

**You're going to learn how to make it okay.**

The first months can be some of the hardest days of your life. There may be pain, phantom sensations, difficult prosthetic fittings, falls, setbacks, and days when you wonder whether you'll ever feel like yourself again.

But believe me when I tell you this:

**It gets better.**

There will come a day when you stand without holding onto anything.

There will come a day when you take a step without thinking about every movement.

And there will come a day when you get so used to your prosthetic that you forget it's even there—until you bump it into something and remember.

And someday, you may even laugh about some of this.

For me, humor became one of the ways I took back control. Everybody finds their own way of dealing with what happened. Maybe yours will be humor. Maybe it's riding. Maybe it's family, faith, art, work, or helping someone else who is going through the same thing.

**Find your way.**

Most importantly, **find your people.**

Find other amputees. Talk to people who have actually lived it. They can tell you things that doctors, therapists, and people who have never experienced limb loss simply can't.

They'll show you what's possible.

That's what the Lost Limb Riders means to me. We're not here to feel sorry for ourselves. We're here to **ride, live, laugh, advocate, and show the world that losing a limb doesn't mean losing your life.**
