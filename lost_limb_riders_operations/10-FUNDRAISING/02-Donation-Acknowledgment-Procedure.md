## **1. Purpose**

This procedure ensures donors receive accurate, IRS-compliant acknowledgment and that the organization keeps contemporaneous records. Confirm current IRS acknowledgment rules annually.

## **2. Acknowledgment Requirements (Federal Notes)**

- **Cash/check/card contributions:** written acknowledgment generally required for single contributions of $250 or more (confirm current IRS threshold).
- **In-kind contributions:** generally required for non-cash contributions of $250 or more; for contributions over $500, the donor generally needs the organization's written acknowledgment with a description (value confirmed by the donor's valuation); over $5,000, appraisals are generally required (donor's responsibility) — confirm current IRS rules.
- **Quid pro quo:** if goods/services were provided, the acknowledgment states the value of the benefit (generally required when the payment exceeds $75).
- **No benefit provided:** the acknowledgment may state "no goods or services were provided in exchange."

## **3. Acknowledgment Content**

Every written acknowledgment includes:

- Organization name
- Donor name and date
- Amount of cash contribution (or description + good-faith estimate of value for in-kind)
- Statement of whether goods/services were provided and their value (if any)
- If restricted: the stated restriction
- Organization contact and EIN (as appropriate)

## **4. Process**

1. Log the donation (DON-YYYY-###) with acknowledgment status at receipt (FUND-PROC-001).
2. Issue the acknowledgment within the organization's standard timeline (e.g., within 30 days; confirm donor needs).
3. Track acknowledgment status in the donor record.
4. For in-kind contributions, complete the in-kind record (description, date, donor, estimated FMV, purpose).
5. For event/sponsorship payments, apply FUND-REF-001 to determine the acknowledgment type.

## **5. Public Inspection Note**

Acknowledgment templates are public; completed acknowledgments are donor records and are stored restricted (REC-LOC-001).
