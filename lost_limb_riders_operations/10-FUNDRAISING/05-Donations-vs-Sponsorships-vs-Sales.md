## **1. Purpose**

Donations, sponsorships, and sales are **not** the same transaction and are not treated identically. This reference helps staff classify revenue correctly. The distinctions affect tax treatment, acknowledgment, and unrelated business income.

## **2. Classification Guide**

| Transaction | What It Is | Tax Treatment Notes | Acknowledgment | Unrelated Business Income (UBI) Note |
|-------------|-----------|----------------------|----------------|---------------------------------------|
| **Donation** | Gift with no substantial benefit to donor | Charitable contribution (deductible per donor's situation) | Written acknowledgment per IRS rules | Not UBI |
| **Sponsorship** | Payment for advertising/promotional benefits | Generally NOT a charitable contribution; may be deductible as a business expense by the sponsor; may have UBI implications (see below) | Quid-pro-quo statement with benefit value | Payments for advertising may be UBI; qualified sponsorship payments generally are not (confirm current rules) |
| **Sale** | Exchange of goods/services (tickets, merchandise, admission) | Purchase price, not a contribution (portion may be contribution if stated) | Receipt; contribution portion acknowledged if applicable | Regularly carried-on trade/business activity may be UBI |

## **3. Application Rules**

1. **Identify the true nature first.** If the payer receives substantial benefits (recognition, advertising, admission, product), it is not a pure donation.
2. **Event tickets:** the ticket price is generally a purchase; any portion stated as a contribution is acknowledged as such (quid pro quo).
3. **Sponsorships:** follow FUND-SPON-001; state benefits delivered; record the value; the sponsor's payment is generally not a charitable contribution to the sponsor.
4. **Never promise** a donor or sponsor a specific tax result. Refer them to their own tax advisor.
5. **When in doubt:** classify as a sale or sponsorship (not a donation) and ask counsel/CPA.

## **4. UBI Reminder**

Income from a regularly carried-on trade or business unrelated to mission may be unrelated business taxable income (UBTI). Advertising income and merchandise sales can create UBI. The Board and CPA should review revenue streams at year-end. This reference is not a legal interpretation; confirm current IRS rules.
