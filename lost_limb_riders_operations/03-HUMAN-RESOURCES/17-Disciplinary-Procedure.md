## **1. Purpose**

This procedure provides fair, consistent, documented corrective action. It protects the employee's due-process expectations and the organization's legal position.

## **2. Principles**

- Corrective action is job-related, consistent, and documented.
- The employee is told what the issue is and given an opportunity to respond.
- Each step is recorded and filed in the disciplinary file (HR-FIL-001, section 2.5).
- Nothing here limits the organization's at-will authority where employment is at will, subject to employment agreements and law.

## **3. Progression (as appropriate to severity)**

1. **Verbal counseling** — documented in a note (date, issue, expectations).
2. **Written warning** — signed by supervisor and employee; states the issue, expected change, and consequences of non-compliance.
3. **Corrective action plan** — specific goals, timeline, and support.
4. **Suspension / final written warning** — for serious or repeated issues, consistent with policy.
5. **Termination** — per HR-SEP-001, following HR-CHK-001 (Checklist C — final payroll).

**Serious misconduct** (theft, fraud, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, safety violation endangering others, falsification of records, violence) may result in immediate suspension or termination regardless of prior steps. Incidents that may be criminal or that trigger whistleblower protections are referred appropriately.

## **4. Documentation**

Every step includes: date, facts (not opinions), witnesses, what was communicated, employee response, next steps, and follow-up dates. Medical or confidential information stays in the separate medical file.

## **5. Appeals**

An employee may request review by the Executive Director (or Board, if the Executive Director took the action). The reviewer responds in writing.
