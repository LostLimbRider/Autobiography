## **1. Purpose**

This policy defines what counts as an organizational record, who owns it, where it lives, and how it is protected. It makes "someone handled it" insufficient: every material transaction must produce a record that can be located and produced on request.

## **2. What Is a Record**

A record is any completed documentation that evidences a transaction, decision, obligation, or activity. Examples: minutes, resolutions, signed forms, time records, expense reports, receipts, invoices, contracts, payroll records, donation logs, grant files, incident reports, asset register entries, and compliance filings.

A policy is not a record. A checklist is not a record until it is completed and signed. The completed, signed version is the record.

The Records & Compliance Officer owns the overall records system and the Master Transaction Register.

## **4. Record Location**

- Blank templates and policies live in this repository.
- Completed records live in the restricted locations defined in `14-RECORDS-MANAGEMENT/01-Records-Location-Register.md` (secure filing, encrypted drives, approved providers).
- No completed confidential record is stored in a public GitHub repository. This includes SSNs, bank accounts, completed W-4/I-9, medical information, private employee/donor/participant records, passwords, credentials, and confidential contracts.

## **5. Record Integrity**

- Records must be accurate, timely, complete, and legible.
- Do not alter a completed record to conceal or misrepresent. Corrections are made as a dated line-through plus initial, or an amended version that supersedes.
- Electronic records are backed up per the Records Location Register.
- Retention and destruction follow CMP-RET-001 and the Document Retention and Destruction Policy. Legal holds suspend destruction.

## **6. Transaction Traceability**

Every transaction is opened with a Transaction ID and entered in the Master Transaction Register (`14-RECORDS-MANAGEMENT/03-Master-Transaction-Register.md`). Related records carry each other's IDs. An auditor must be able to follow any expenditure from authorization to supporting documentation.

## **7. Access and Confidentiality**

- Access to restricted records is limited to authorized personnel on a need-to-know basis.
- Personnel, medical, and I-9 records are kept in separate, restricted files (see HR-FIL-001).
- Donor records are kept confidential per the Privacy Policy.
- No public disclosure of restricted information without a signed release or legal obligation.

## **8. Public Inspection**

Documents subject to IRS public-inspection rules (e.g., Form 990, exemption application) are kept available as required by law. See CMP-IRS-001.
