## 1. Organization Naming

- **Full legal name** -- Lost Limb Riders (no "Inc." unless referencing a specific legal filing)
- **Short form** -- Lost Limb Riders or LLR (internal use only)
- **Never use** -- "the Limb Riders," "Lost Limb," "LLR Inc." (unless in legal filing), or any variation not listed here
- **First reference in document** -- Full name: "Lost Limb Riders"
- **Subsequent references** -- "the Organization," "the Organization," "Lost Limb Riders," or "LLR" (internal documents only)
- **Possessive** -- Lost Limb Riders' (no apostrophe-s for organizations ending in s)

## 3. Participant, Member, and Rider Terminology

- Term: Participant | Definition: Any individual receiving services, attending programs, or engaging with Lost Limb Riders programs | When to Use: Default term for program documentation, intake forms, assessments, outcome tracking
- Term: Member | Definition: Community designation for participants, riders, volunteers, families, and supporters who identify with the organization | When to Use: Use in community-facing materials, handbooks, welcome letters, social media. Do not imply statutory membership rights (see Bylaws Article III)
- Term: Rider | Definition: A person who rides or wants to ride motorcycles in connection with Lost Limb Riders events and culture | When to Use: Use in ride-specific materials, motorcycle event planning, motorcycle cultural context
- Term: Amputee | Definition: A person who has undergone amputation | When to Use: Use in medical, clinical, and factual contexts. Acceptable in all contexts when referring to the condition.
- Term: Limb-different individual | Definition: A person with a congenital limb difference or acquired limb variation | When to Use: Use alongside "amputee" when the full population is being described
- Term: Volunteer | Definition: A person who provides unpaid service to the organization | When to Use: Use in operational documentation, role descriptions, time tracking
- Term: Donor | Definition: A person or entity providing financial or in-kind support | When to Use: Use in fundraising materials, stewardship records, financial documentation
- Term: Supporter | Definition: General term for anyone who aligns with the mission | When to Use: Use in general communications, social media, casual reference

**Rule:** In formal program documents, use "participants." In community-facing materials, "members" is acceptable. Never use "victims," "patients" (unless in clinical referral context), "sufferers," or "afflicted."

## 4. Leadership Titles

- **Founder and Chief Executive Officer (Founder/CEO)** -- John Thompson's title when serving in the combined founding and executive role. Use in formal correspondence, press materials, grant applications, and partner communications.
- **Executive Director (ED)** -- The operational executive if appointed separately from the Founder/CEO. Use in operational contexts.
- **Chairperson** -- Head of the Board of Directors. Not "Chairman" or "President."
- **Vice Chairperson** -- Vice head of the Board.
- **Secretary** -- Board officer responsible for records and governance documentation.
- **Treasurer** -- Board officer responsible for financial governance.
- **Program Director** -- Operational lead for Family Support Initiative and related programs.
- **Volunteer Coordinator** -- Operational lead for volunteer recruitment, training, and management.
- **Board Member / Director** -- Any voting member of the Board of Directors.

**Rules:**

- Use full titles on first reference in formal documents.
- Abbreviations (ED, PD) may be used after first reference.
- Never use "Chairman" — use "Chairperson."
- Never use "President" for board officers — use "Chairperson."
- "Founder" alone may be used in narrative and biographical contexts when referring to John Thompson.

## 5. Motto and Tagline Usage

- Term: Motto | Text: "I Can. I Will." | Context: Personal motto of the founder. Inspirational context, merchandise, personal communications, closing statements.
- Term: Tagline | Text: "Nobody Is Left Behind. Nobody Stands Alone." | Context: Organizational tagline. All organizational materials, marketing, signage, event materials, presentations.
- Term: Combined | Text: "I Can. I Will. Nobody Is Left Behind. Nobody Stands Alone." | Context: Acceptable in closing statements of speeches and presentations.

**Rules:**

- Motto uses first person ("I"). Tagline uses third person ("Nobody").
- Do not modify either phrase.
- Do not use "We Can. We Will." in official materials unless specifically approved by the founder for a particular context.
- Both motto and tagline may appear on merchandise, signage, and printed materials.

## 6. Disability and Limb Loss Terminology

- Preferred: Amputee | Avoid: "Amputated person," "person who got cut" | Reason: "Amputee" is the accepted self-identifier in the limb-loss community
- Preferred: Limb-different individual | Avoid: "Deformed," "handicapped," "crippled" | Reason: Person-first, respectful, accurate
- Preferred: Person with a disability | Avoid: "Disabled person" (unless used as identity-first by the individual) | Reason: Person-first is the organizational standard; respect individual preference
- Preferred: Limb loss | Avoid: "Losing a limb," "lost his/her leg" (in formal docs) | Reason: "Limb loss" is the clinical and community standard term
- Preferred: Prosthetic limb / prosthetic | Avoid: "Fake leg," "artificial limb" (in formal docs) | Reason: Clinical and respectful
- Preferred: Adaptive equipment | Avoid: "Special equipment," "handicapped gear" | Reason: Neutral, accurate
- Preferred: Uses a wheelchair / wheelchair user | Avoid: "Wheelchair-bound," "confined to a wheelchair" | Reason: Person-first, accurate (wheelchairs provide freedom, not confinement)
- Preferred: Accessible parking / accessible entrance | Avoid: "Handicapped parking," "handicapped entrance" | Reason: Current ADA and community standard
- Preferred: Survivor | Avoid: "Victim" (except in legal/criminal context) | Reason: Empowering, accurate for most contexts

**Additional rules:**

- Do not use language that implies pity, tragedy, or inspiration solely because of disability.
- Do not describe people as "suffering from" limb loss unless quoting a medical source.
- Respect individual identity preferences — some people prefer identity-first language ("amputee" is itself identity-first and is the community standard).
- In the manuscript and personal narrative, use John's natural voice. This style guide applies to organizational documents.

## 7. Document Formatting Standards

- **Headings** -- Use `# **Title**` format for document titles and chapter headings
- **Subheadings** -- Use `## **Section**` and `### **Subsection**`
- **Lists** -- Use `-` for unordered lists, `1.` for ordered lists
- **Emphasis** -- Use `**bold**` for key terms, definitions, and section labels. Use `_italic_` for document titles and minor emphasis.
- **Blockquotes** -- Use `>` for direct quotes, mission statements when excerpted, and founder statements
- **Horizontal rules** -- Do not use `---` inside chapter content. Acceptable in front matter, title pages, and non-manuscript documents.
- **Apostrophes and quotes** -- Use curly/smart quotes throughout: ' ' " " (never straight ' ")
- **Em dashes** -- Use — (em dash, U+2014)
- **Non-breaking hyphens** -- Use ‑ (U+2011) in compound modifiers: "limb‑loss," "peer‑driven," "well‑being"
- **File encoding** -- UTF-8 only

## 8. Cross-References and Authority Chains

All organizational documents should identify their place in the authority chain:

- **Bylaws** -- Iowa Code Chapter 504, Articles of Incorporation
- **Governance Policies** -- Bylaws
- **Committee Charters** -- Bylaws Article VI, Governance Policies
- **Program Manuals** -- Bylaws, Governance Framework, DOCUMENT-CONTROLLED-MISSION-STATEMENT.md
- **Public Materials (Handbook, About, Proposal)** -- DOCUMENT-CONTROLLED-MISSION-STATEMENT.md
- **Manuscript** -- Author's voice — this style guide does not govern manuscript content

When a document references governance terms (board size, committees, financial thresholds, policies), it should state that the Bylaws or Governance Framework controls in case of discrepancy.
