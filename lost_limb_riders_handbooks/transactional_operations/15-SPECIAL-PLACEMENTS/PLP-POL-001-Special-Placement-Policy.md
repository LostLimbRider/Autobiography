## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the governing rules for special placements — work-study, community service, court-ordered, internship, practicum, workforce, work-experience, vocational, school, and agency placements. It keeps special placements distinct from employees and volunteers and bounds their authority.

## 2. Policy

- **Distinct status.** Special-placement participants are not employees and (unless they meet the ordinary-volunteer test in VOL-PROC-001) are not ordinary volunteers. They serve under a defined placement controlled by PLP-PROC-001.
- **Classification first.** The actual relationship and applicable law determine classification (employee, volunteer, or special placement). No person performs work until classification and approval are done (PLP-PROC-001, §6 and §9).
- **No coercion.** No one is pressured to accept a placement to retain wages, employment, or benefits. Service is freely given except where a court or program lawfully requires it.
- **External obligations honored.** Court, agency, school, workforce, and program requirements are verified and honored. Lost Limb Riders personnel never alter a court’s requirements.
- **No employee substitution.** The organization shall not use a special placement to replace an employee role to avoid the employment process. Any such arrangement is escalated before work begins.
- **Bound authority.** A participant’s authority is limited to the documented duty authorization (PLP-FORM-006). Participants do not bind the organization, sign contracts, authorize spending, hire or remove staff, or access restricted systems unless expressly authorized.
- **Records.** Placement records are confidential (GOV-POL-002), stored per REC-REF-001, and retained per REC-MATRIX-001. Completed placement records are never placed in the public repository.
- **Safety.** Participants receive orientation and safety instruction (PLP-PROC-001 §12–13); incidents follow SAF-POL-002.

## 3. When Placement Appears to Be Employment

A placement that occupies a paid staff role, is regularly supervised and ongoing, resembles an employee’s duties, or substitutes for a paid role is not a special placement. It must be escalated (PLP-PROC-001 §6). The organization shall not misclassify an employee as a placement.
