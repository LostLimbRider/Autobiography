## 1. Purpose

Every controlled document must be identifiable, versioned, approved, and traceable. This policy establishes the document control system so that no one depends on institutional memory to know which version of a requirement applies.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all organizational policies, procedures, standard operating procedures, checklists, forms, registers, and templates used to run the organization — including all documents in the transactional operations layer.

## 3. Document IDs

Every controlled document has a unique **Document ID** in the format `DEPT-TYPE-NNN` (see MASTER-INDEX.md §5). IDs are assigned sequentially within each department’s series and are never reused.

## 5. Document Types

| Type | Code | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| Policy | POL | What the organization requires |
| Standard Operating Procedure | SOP | How a recurring task is performed |
| Procedure | PROC | Detailed how-to for a workflow |
| Checklist | CHK | What the operator must verify |
| Form | FORM | What data is captured |
| Register | REG | What recurring transactions are tracked |
| Template | TMP | Reusable letter or document shell |
| Reference | REF | Index, map, or guidance |

Use one consistent convention; do not mix.

## 6. Versioning

- Version numbering starts at 1.0 and increments for substantive changes (1.0 → 1.1 → 2.0).
- A new version **supersedes** the prior version. The replacement sets `Supersedes` to the prior Document ID; the prior document is marked superseded.
- Superseded documents are retained (not deleted) in the location designated by Records Management and are no longer used for operations.

## 7. Approval

- Policies require Board approval unless the Board delegates otherwise.
- Procedures, SOPs, checklists, forms, and templates require the approval required by the approval matrix (FIN-CTRL-001) — typically the responsible department head or Executive Director.
- Emergency changes may be approved by Executive Leadership and must be ratified at the next Board meeting (consistent with the Organization Handbook document-control requirements).

## 8. Review

Every controlled document has a review date, normally within 12 months. The owner reviews the document, verifies it remains accurate, and either renews it (new review date) or initiates a change.

## 9. Linkage

Every document links to its governing policy, its procedure, its checklist, its forms, and its registers. Every transaction produced by a form links to accounting, record storage, and retention. No form exists without a procedure; no procedure exists without a policy.

## 10. Violations

Operating without an approved, current document where one is required is a control deficiency and must be reported through the change-control process.
