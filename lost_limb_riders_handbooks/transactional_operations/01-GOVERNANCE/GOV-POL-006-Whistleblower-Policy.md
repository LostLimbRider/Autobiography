## 1. Purpose

Employees, volunteers, and board members must be able to report suspected misconduct — fraud, waste, abuse, financial impropriety, policy violations, or unlawful activity — without fear of retaliation.

## 2. What to Report

- Fraud, theft, embezzlement, or misuse of organizational assets
- Falsified records, time sheets, receipts, or reports
- Conflicts of interest or excess benefit arrangements
- Misuse of restricted funds or grant money
- Harassment, discrimination, or retaliation
- Illegal or unsafe activity

## 3. How to Report

Reports may be made to:

1. the immediate supervisor, or
2. the Executive Director, or
3. the Board Chairperson, or
4. any Board member.

Reporters may remain anonymous where lawful and practical. The report is logged and handled confidentially to the extent possible.

## 4. Handling

- The Board (or a Board-designated committee) reviews credible reports.
- Investigations are prompt, confidential, and impartial.
- Substantiated findings are remediated per SAF-POL-001, including correcting the transaction, recovering funds where appropriate, disciplinary action, and reporting to authorities where required.

## 5. Non-Retaliation

Retaliation against a good-faith reporter is prohibited and is itself a violation subject to discipline. Reporters who act in good faith are protected even if the report turns out to be unsubstantiated.

## 6. Documentation

Reports, investigation notes, and outcomes are recorded in restricted records, not in the public repository.
