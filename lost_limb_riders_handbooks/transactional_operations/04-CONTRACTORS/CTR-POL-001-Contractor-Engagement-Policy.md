## 1. Purpose

To engage independent contractors lawfully, document the relationship, and pay them properly — while preventing misclassification of workers who are actually employees.

## 2. Warning

**Calling someone a contractor does not make them one.**

Worker classification is a legal determination based on the facts of the relationship (how much control the organization exercises, whether the work is part of the regular business, whether the person has an independent business, etc.) under IRS and wage-law standards. Misclassifying an employee as a contractor can result in unpaid taxes, penalties, wage claims, and back benefits.

## 3. When a Contractor May Be Used

An independent contractor may be engaged when the person:

- controls how and when the work is performed;
- provides their own tools, equipment, and methods;
- offers services to the public or other clients;
- is paid by the job/project rather than by time;
- does not receive employee benefits, training, or day-to-day supervision;
- and the arrangement satisfies the factors in CTR-CHK-001.

## 4. Required Documentation

Every contractor engagement requires, before work begins:

1. Completed classification review (CTR-CHK-001)
2. W-9 collected (CTR-CHK-002)
3. Signed written agreement (CTR-FORM-001)
4. Scope of work (CTR-FORM-002)
5. Approved rate and payment terms
6. Payment authorization path (CTR-FORM-004 / FIN-PROC-002)

No work, no payment.

## 5. Payments and Reporting

- Contractors are paid by invoice (CTR-FORM-003) after verification of completed work.
- No federal or Iowa income tax is withheld from contractors; the contractor is responsible for their own taxes.
- Payments meeting the threshold are reported on Form 1099-NEC at year end (CTR-SOP-001), per current IRS rules.

## 6. Prohibited

- Treating a worker as a contractor to avoid payroll taxes.
- Contracting with someone for work that is core, supervised, ongoing organization work without a valid classification basis.
- Paying contractors without a W-9 and agreement on file.
- Engaging a contractor without checking for conflicts of interest (GOV-POL-004).

## 7. Records

Contractor records (agreement, W-9, invoices, 1099 records) are retained per REC-MATRIX-001. W-9s contain taxpayer ID numbers and are stored in restricted records (REC-REF-001).
