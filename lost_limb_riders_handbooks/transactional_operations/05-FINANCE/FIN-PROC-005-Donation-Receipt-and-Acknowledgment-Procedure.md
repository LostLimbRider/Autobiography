## 1. Purpose

To record every donation and to issue acknowledgments that meet IRS requirements so donors can substantiate charitable contributions and the organization can support its records.

## 2. Workflow

```text
Donation Received → Identify Donor → Determine Restriction → Record → Deposit
→ Acknowledge → Donor Record → Restricted Fund Tracking → Reconcile
```

## 3. Recording

Every donation is recorded promptly in FIN-REG-002 with donor, amount, date, restriction (if any), payment method, campaign, and acknowledgment status. Cash is counted by two people (FIN-POL-002). A donation receives `DON-YYYY-NNN`.

## 4. Acknowledgment Rules (Federal)

- **$250 or more:** a contemporaneous written acknowledgment is required for the donor to claim a deduction. It must state the amount contributed and whether the organization provided any goods or services in return, with a good-faith estimate of their value (confirm current IRS rules in CMP-IRS-001).
- **Under $250:** a receipt or acknowledgment is good practice; include the amount and date.
- **In-kind donations:** describe the property and whether any goods/services were provided in exchange (FIN-FORM-008).
- **Quid pro quo contributions above the threshold:** disclosure statement required when a donor gives more than the value received.

## 5. What NOT to Say

- Do not assign a value to donated goods or services (the donor determines value for their deduction).
- Do not state a donation is deductible as a charitable contribution unless it is; the organization does not provide tax advice.
- Do not acknowledge a donation before it is recorded.

## 6. Procedure

1. Record the donation (FIN-REG-002) with restriction noted.
2. Deposit per FIN-CHK-004; log on FIN-FORM-007.
3. Issue the acknowledgment (FIN-FORM-006) within a defined timeframe (good practice: within 30 days; **before** the donor’s filing deadline for ≥$250).
4. File acknowledgment copies with the donation record.
5. Track restricted amounts in FIN-REG-003 and spend only per the restriction (FIN-PROC-006).
6. Reconcile donation records to deposits and bank statements monthly.

## 7. Exceptions

- **Anonymous gifts:** recorded without donor identity; still acknowledged generically if needed.
- **Sponsorships** (payments for advertising/promotional benefits) are **not** treated as charitable donations — see FUND-POL-001.
- **Event ticket/donation splits:** when an event fee exceeds the value of goods/services, the excess may be a donation; document the split per applicable rules and the event file.
