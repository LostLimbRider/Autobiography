## 1. Purpose

Sponsorships bring in revenue and promotional partners; each is documented in a written agreement that defines the amount and the benefits (promotional value) provided, and is recorded correctly.

## 2. Key Distinction

A sponsorship where the sponsor receives advertising or promotional benefits is generally **not** a tax-deductible charitable contribution for the sponsor. Lost Limb Riders:

- Records sponsorship revenue as program/fundraising revenue, **not** as a donation.
- Provides the sponsor a written agreement (FUND-SPON-001) describing the amount and benefits.
- Does **not** represent the sponsorship as tax-deductible in acknowledgment wording.
- For any gift exceeding the value of benefits provided, treats the excess per the quid-pro-quo rules (FIN-PROC-005) and documents the split.

## 3. Procedure

1. **Prospect and proposal.** Identify the sponsor; prepare the proposal (benefits, amount, audience, timing).
2. **Negotiate.** Agree on amount and benefits; confirm benefits are deliverable.
3. **Agreement.** Execute FUND-SPON-001 (with authority per FIN-CTRL-001; major sponsorships to the Board).
4. **Invoice/collect.** Bill per the agreement; record `SPN-YYYY-NNN` in FIN-REG-006; deposit per FIN-CHK-004.
5. **Deliver.** Provide the agreed promotional benefits; document delivery.
6. **Acknowledge.** Provide the agreement/benefit confirmation; do not claim deductibility improperly (FIN-PROC-005).
7. **Close and reconcile.** Reconcile revenue, benefits delivered, and any unfulfilled obligations at event closeout (EVT-CLOSE-001).

## 4. Prohibitions

- Providing benefits the organization cannot deliver.
- Misrepresenting the tax treatment of the sponsorship.
- Recording sponsorship as a charitable donation in FIN-REG-002.
