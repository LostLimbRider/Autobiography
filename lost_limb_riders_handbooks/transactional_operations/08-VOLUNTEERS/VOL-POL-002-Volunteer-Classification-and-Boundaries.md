## 1. Purpose

This policy keeps the distinction between employees and volunteers, and it bounds volunteer authority. An individual is classified according to the actual nature of the relationship and applicable requirements, not according to what the organization wishes the label to be.

## 2. Employee vs. Volunteer

An **employee** occupies an authorized position and is subject to the organization’s employment, compensation, supervision, personnel, onboarding, performance, and separation procedures (HR packet).

A **volunteer** provides service to the organization without being placed into an employee position. A volunteer does not become an employee merely because the volunteer:

- performs substantial work;
- works regularly;
- has assigned responsibilities;
- reports to a supervisor;
- works scheduled hours;
- receives orientation or training;
- works independently;
- holds a title;
- or participates in organizational programs.

Conversely, an employee must not be improperly treated as a volunteer simply to avoid the employee process. Classification is determined by the actual nature of the arrangement. When classification is uncertain, leadership is briefed **before** the person is assigned duties, and legal guidance is obtained when warranted.

## 3. Volunteer Authority Limits

A volunteer’s authority is limited to the authority specifically granted for the assignment. A volunteer does not automatically have authority to:

- bind the organization;
- sign contracts;
- authorize expenditures;
- hire or remove employees;
- establish or alter official policy;
- represent themselves as organizational leadership;
- access restricted or financial information or systems;
- make commitments on the organization’s behalf;
- assign employees duties outside their role.

If a volunteer needs authority beyond the defined assignment, it must be escalated to the appropriate organizational authority (FIN-CTRL-001).

## 4. Volunteers Supervising Volunteers or Employees

A volunteer supervises another volunteer **only when that supervisory responsibility has been expressly authorized** and recorded in the assignment. The supervising volunteer must understand what authority they hold, what they do not, who they report to, and what issues must be escalated.

A volunteer does not supervise an employee unless the authorized structure expressly provides for it and the arrangement is formally approved. Informal statements such as “you’re in charge today” create no permanent authority.

## 5. No Informal Bypass

The following do **not** create a completed volunteer assignment:

- “John said it’s okay.”
- “The coordinator knows them.”
- “They’ve volunteered before.”
- “They came to the event and started helping.”
- “Everybody knows they’re a volunteer.”
- “We’ll do the paperwork later.”

Informal authorization never replaces the process (VOL-PROC-001). Emergency situations may use an approved emergency procedure, with documentation completed afterward.

## 6. Boundary Review

Leadership performs a boundary review whenever a proposed assignment begins to resemble an employee role (see VOL-PROC-001). The organization shall not use volunteer status to avoid employment obligations.
