## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes a complete, documented, repeatable volunteer assignment process for Lost Limb Riders. A person who expresses interest in volunteering is **not** automatically an active volunteer assignment. Every volunteer assignment must have a documented beginning, authorization, defined responsibilities, supervision, accountability, and conclusion.

For every volunteer assignment, the organization must be able to answer:

- Who is the volunteer?
- What are they authorized to do?
- Where are they assigned?
- Who approved the assignment?
- Who supervises them?
- What requirements were completed?
- When did the assignment begin?
- What records were created?
- What happened during the assignment?
- When and why did the assignment end?

No portion of this process runs on informal verbal understanding. It ends with a documented transaction record and a defined closeout state.

## 2. Employee and Volunteer Are Distinct

Volunteer status is distinct from employee status (VOL-POL-002 and HR-EMP-001).

- An **employee** occupies an authorized paid position and is subject to the employment, compensation, supervision, personnel, onboarding, performance, and separation procedures in the HR packet.
- A **volunteer** provides service to the organization without being placed into an employee position.

A volunteer does not become an employee merely because the volunteer performs substantial work, works regularly, has assigned responsibilities, reports to a supervisor, works scheduled hours, receives orientation or training, works independently, holds a title, or participates in organizational programs. Conversely, an employee must not be treated as a volunteer to avoid the employee process. Classification is decided on the actual nature of the relationship. When classification is uncertain, escalate to organizational leadership **before** duties are assigned.

Whenever a proposed assignment begins to resemble an employee position, leadership must perform the boundary review at this procedure and obtain professional or legal guidance when uncertain.

## 4. Employee – Volunteer Boundary Review

Apply a boundary review whenever a proposed volunteer role shows any of these warning signs:

- regular, recurring work that looks like a staff position
- duties equivalent to an established paid position
- expectations that resemble employment
- significant organizational control over the person’s work
- compensation or benefits
- responsibility that should belong to a paid role
- use of the volunteer label to avoid employment obligations

If any sign applies, escalate to the Executive Director **before** the person performs the assignment. The organization shall not intentionally use volunteer status to avoid employment obligations. When uncertain, obtain professional or legal guidance.

## 7. Stage Definitions and Execution

### Interest and Initial Contact (VOL-FORM-002)

Interest does not equal approval. When a person expresses interest, record the inquiry on VOL-FORM-002: name, contact information, date, method of contact, area of interest, program or activity, the person receiving the inquiry, initial notes, and next action. The person is not represented as an approved active volunteer until the required process is complete.

### Application and Review (VOL-FORM-001)

The person submits the Volunteer Application (VOL-FORM-001), dated. An authorized reviewer determines whether it is complete, whether more information is required, whether screening applies, and whether the applicant should proceed. The reviewer records a disposition:

```text
APPROVED TO PROCEED
PENDING ADDITIONAL INFORMATION
SCREENING REQUIRED
NOT APPROVED
WITHDRAWN
```

No assignment proceeds while required information remains outstanding.

### Screening (VOL-APP-001, VOL-FORM-003)

Screening depends on the role: access to vulnerable individuals, transportation, confidential or financial access, organizational property, unsupervised activity, and lawful or program-specific requirements. The screener records whether screening was required, what applied, completion date, disposition, and follow-up. Sensitive screening information is restricted (GOV-POL-002) and not distributed.

### Approval (VOL-FORM-004)

A designated authority approves the person for service. The approval record identifies the volunteer, the approving authority, the date, the approved capacity or program, and any restrictions or conditions. Approval to volunteer does not automatically authorize every activity.

### Record Created (VOL-CHK-002)

Upon approval the Coordinator opens or activates the volunteer file and logs the volunteer in VOL-REG-001.

### Orientation (VOL-FORM-005)

Before service the volunteer receives orientation on mission, expectations, responsibilities, confidentiality, conduct, safety, communication, boundaries, reporting, chain of responsibility, prohibited conduct, and emergency procedures. Acknowledgment is dated.

### Assignment Planning and Authorization (VOL-FORM-006, VOL-FORM-007)

A volunteer is never told “Go help with whatever needs to be done.” The role and duties are defined on VOL-FORM-006 (volunteer, program, title, purpose, duties, boundaries, location, period, schedule, supervisor, training, equipment, reporting) and are authorized on VOL-FORM-007 **before** service unless an approved emergency procedure applies. A volunteer may not independently create their own assignment.

### Training (VOL-FORM-008)

Required training is completed before the volunteer performs duties requiring it and is documented on VOL-FORM-008.

### Authorized Active Assignment

An assignment is only active when all of the following are complete:

```text
Approved
+ Required screening complete
+ Orientation complete
+ Required training complete
+ Assignment authorized
+ Supervisor assigned
= Authorized active volunteer assignment
```

### Authority Limits and Supervision

A volunteer’s authority is limited to the assignment (VOL-POL-002). Volunteers do not bind the organization, sign contracts, authorize expenditures, hire or fire, set policy, or access restricted systems unless specifically authorized. Every assignment has a named supervisor who communicates expectations, provides direction, confirms training, monitors participation, answers questions, addresses problems, documents significant issues, and reports incidents. A volunteer supervises another volunteer only when expressly authorized and recorded. A volunteer does not supervise an employee unless the structure expressly provides for it and the arrangement is formally approved; “you’re in charge today” creates no permanent authority.

### Participation and Hours (VOL-FORM-009)

Participation is recorded in VOL-FORM-009 (date, activity, start and end, hours, location, program, supervisor, notes). Hours are not wages.

### Communication

Volunteers communicate missed commitments, problems, safety concerns, incidents, and requests for clarification. Supervisors respond to material issues and document significant matters.

### Assignment Changes (VOL-FORM-010)

Material changes to duties, authority, supervisor, location, program, schedule, access, training, or duration are documented on VOL-FORM-010 and re-approved if required. A volunteer may not accrue responsibilities informally until the role no longer resembles the approved assignment.

### Incidents (SAF-FORM-001)

Significant incidents are documented per SAF-POL-002 on SAF-FORM-001: date, people involved, factual description, immediate actions, person notified, follow-up, and resolution when known.

### Performance and Conduct

Issues are handled proportionately. The supervisor identifies the concern, discusses it, clarifies expectations, documents significant concerns, sets corrective expectations, monitors, and escalates when necessary. Not every problem requires formal discipline.

### Suspension (VOL-FORM-011)

A volunteer may be suspended on safety, investigation, or conduct grounds. The suspension records date, reason, authority, whether the volunteer may perform any other duties, return conditions, and follow-up responsibility.

### Completion, Withdrawal, and Termination (VOL-FORM-012, 013, 014)

The coordinator records the correct end state. These are not necessarily the same event:

```text
Assignment completed (VOL-FORM-012)
Volunteer withdrew (VOL-FORM-013)
Assignment suspended (VOL-FORM-011)
Assignment ended by organization (VOL-FORM-014)
Volunteer relationship ended
```

Withdrawal reason is recorded only when freely given.

### Access and Property Return (VOL-FORM-015)

When an assignment ends, the coordinator determines whether any access, keys, equipment, records, credentials, or program access must be returned or revoked, and records the outcome.

### Record Closure, Retention, and Reactivation (VOL-CHK-002)

The record is reviewed and closed. Closeout confirms assignment disposition, required documentation, hours, incidents, property, access, outstanding obligations, and retention classification (REC-MATRIX-001). A former volunteer does not automatically regain prior authorizations. On reactivation the Coordinator reassesses whether the existing record applies, whether screening and training must be renewed, whether orientation must be refreshed, and whether a new authorization is required.

## 8. No Informal Bypass

The following do **not** constitute an active assignment:

- “John said it’s okay.”
- “The coordinator knows them.”
- “They’ve volunteered before.”
- “They came to the event and started helping.”
- “Everybody knows they’re a volunteer.”
- “We’ll fill the paperwork out later.”

Informal approval does not replace authorization. Emergencies may use an approved emergency procedure, but the required documents are completed afterward.

## 9. Final Operational Standard

The process must allow a newly appointed leader to take **one real volunteer assignment** and reconstruct its full lifecycle from records: who applied, was reviewed, screened, approved, oriented, assigned, authorized, trained, supervised, participated, had changes and issues documented, and who completed, withdrew, was suspended, or was removed — and that the record was closed. That is the minimum standard for calling the process complete.

## 10. Management Review

For every stage, leadership classifies the existing documentation using the standard assessment terms:

```text
EXISTS
PARTIALLY EXISTS
MISSING
DUPLICATED
CONFLICTING
NEEDS APPROVAL
NEEDS REVISION
```

A procedure is complete only when a real assignment can be executed end to end using this process without guessing what happens next.
